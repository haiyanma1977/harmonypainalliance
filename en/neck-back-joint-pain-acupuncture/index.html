Acupuncture for Neck, Back & Joint Pain

A growing interest in non-drug options for pain

Neck pain, low back pain, sciatica, shoulder pain and sports-related strains can limit how you work, sleep and move. More and more people are looking for non-drug options they can use alongside their regular medical care — and acupuncture is one of them.

This shift is reflected in U.S. medical guidance. The American College of Physicians' 2017 clinical practice guideline recommends that people with chronic low back pain initially choose non-drug treatment, and lists acupuncture among those options (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence for acupuncture).1 Since 2020, Medicare has covered acupuncture for chronic low back pain — up to 12 visits in 90 days, with up to 8 more for patients who show improvement.2

This page summarizes what published research says, what a visit usually involves, and how Harmony Pain Alliance can help you find a licensed acupuncture clinic that focuses on these concerns.

What the research says

Chronic pain overall. A large analysis of individual data from 20,827 patients in 39 randomized trials found that the benefits of acupuncture for chronic pain — including back, neck and shoulder pain — persisted over time, with only a small decrease (about 15%) in treatment effect at one year.3

Sciatica. In a 2024 randomized clinical trial of patients with chronic sciatica from a herniated disk, the acupuncture group reported less pain and better function than the sham group at week 4, and these differences persisted through week 52.4

Low back pain. A 2025 Cochrane overview concluded that, for chronic low back pain, acupuncture probably reduces pain and slightly improves function compared with no treatment, and probably improves function slightly compared with sham acupuncture.5

Neck pain. A 2024 meta-analysis found that acupuncture used alongside other care may provide pain relief lasting at least 3 months after treatment for chronic neck pain, with a good safety profile.6

Shoulder pain. A 2024 meta-analysis of frozen shoulder studies found that combining acupuncture with physical therapy was associated with less pain and better range of motion than physical therapy alone.7

Sports injuries. Research on acupuncture for specific sports injuries is still limited. A licensed practitioner can talk with you about whether acupuncture fits into your recovery plan.

Not every study is positive: in some trials, the difference between real and sham acupuncture was small.8 Research results describe groups of patients, not any one person. Acupuncture is not a substitute for medical evaluation — please see a physician for new, severe or worsening pain, numbness, weakness, or loss of bladder or bowel control.

What to expect at a visit

Most clinics begin with questions about your pain, health history and goals, followed by a physical assessment. Treatment uses very thin, single-use sterile needles; some clinics also use electroacupuncture, cupping or other techniques, depending on your condition. Tell your practitioner about any medical conditions and medications, especially blood thinners.

In Florida, acupuncture is provided by Acupuncture Physicians (AP) licensed by the Florida Department of Health. Insurance and Medicare coverage depend on your plan and on the clinic — ask the clinic and your insurer before your first visit.
