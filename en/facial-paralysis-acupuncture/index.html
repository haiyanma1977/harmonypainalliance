Acupuncture for Bell's Palsy & Facial Paralysis

Facial paralysis and where acupuncture fits

Bell's palsy is a sudden weakness or paralysis of the muscles on one side of the face, with no identifiable cause. It is often temporary, but it can make it hard to close the eye or eat and drink normally, and some people have lasting effects. Not everyone with facial weakness has Bell's palsy, so a prompt medical evaluation comes first.1 If facial drooping comes with arm weakness, trouble speaking or other sudden symptoms, it may be a stroke — call 911 right away.

Medical guidelines recommend steroids for Bell's palsy. The 2013 American Academy of Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery (AAO-HNS) guideline strongly recommends that clinicians prescribe oral steroids within 72 hours of symptom onset for patients 16 and older, and protect the eye when it does not close fully. The same guideline states that "no recommendation can be made" regarding acupuncture for Bell's palsy.1 The 2012 American Academy of Neurology guideline update states that for new-onset Bell's palsy, steroids "are highly likely to be effective" and should be offered.2 Japan's 2023 guideline update reviewed acupuncture as one of its clinical questions. It strongly recommends corticosteroids and states that other treatments are weakly recommended due to insufficient evidence.3

This page summarizes what published research says about acupuncture alongside medical care, and how HPA can help you find licensed clinics that focus on facial paralysis.

What the research says

Electroacupuncture, alone or with usual care. A 2026 meta-analysis of 10 randomized trials (888 participants) with peripheral facial palsy found that treatment regimens including electroacupuncture "may improve short-term clinical response." The authors noted that substantial heterogeneity, low-certainty evidence, variable comparison treatments and limited follow-up prevent firm conclusions.4

Intractable (hard-to-treat) facial paralysis. For intractable facial paralysis, a 2023 meta-analysis of 18 studies (1,119 participants) reported that electroacupuncture was more beneficial than treatment without it. All included studies had some risk of bias, and the authors said there was not enough evidence to evaluate its safety or longer-term effects.5

Compared with medication. A 2024 meta-analysis of 15 studies of Eastern medicine approaches, including acupuncture, found no significant differences in effective and cured rates across treatment groups and was unable to support recommending these approaches for facial paralysis. It did not find any of these approaches — alone, combined, or given with Western medicine — to be more effective than Western medicine alone.6 An earlier 2019 meta-analysis of 11 randomized trials (1,258 participants) comparing acupuncture with drug treatment reported higher cure and overall response rates with acupuncture. The authors concluded that acupuncture "seems to be an effective therapy" for Bell's palsy, while noting there was not enough evidence to prove its safety and that the results should be interpreted cautiously because of the poor quality and heterogeneity of the included studies.7

Not every finding is positive: the Cochrane review of acupuncture for Bell's palsy concluded that the quality of the included trials was inadequate to allow any conclusion about its efficacy.8 Across these reviews, the authors pointed to low-quality or short-term evidence.4,5,6,7,8 Research results describe groups of patients, not any one person. Acupuncture does not replace medical evaluation or treatment. See a physician promptly for new facial weakness. The AAO-HNS guideline advises reassessment or referral to a facial nerve specialist if new or worsening neurological symptoms or eye symptoms develop, or if facial recovery is incomplete 3 months after symptoms began.1

What to expect at a visit

Acupuncture for facial paralysis is used alongside your medical care, not instead of it. Before starting, let your physician know. Tell the licensed Acupuncture Physician (AP) about all your medications and treatments, including steroids. In electroacupuncture, a mild electrical current is passed through some of the needles. Needling can cause minor bleeding or bruising.

In Florida, acupuncture is provided by Acupuncture Physicians (AP) licensed by the Florida Department of Health. Insurance coverage for acupuncture varies by plan — ask the clinic and your insurer before your first visit.
